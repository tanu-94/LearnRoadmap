Production notes with definitions, business intuition, canonical syntax, runnable queries, interview traps, and cross-tool translations.

1. Basic Querying & Row Filtering

SELECT, DISTINCT, LIMIT

DQL

📌 Definition & Meaning: SELECT specifies which columns to fetch. DISTINCT eliminates duplicate rows from results. LIMIT caps row count to safely audit schemas without crashing systems.

💻 Canonical Syntax:
SELECT DISTINCT column1, column2 FROM table_name LIMIT 10;
⚡ Runnable Business Example:
-- Preview top 5 active customer regions
SELECT DISTINCT region 
FROM customer_profiles 
WHERE is_active = 1 
LIMIT 5;

⚠️ Interview Trap: DISTINCT operates on all selected columns combined, not just the first column listed.

🔗 Cross-Tool: Excel: Remove Duplicates / =UNIQUE() | Pandas: df[['col']].drop_duplicates().head(5)

WHERE with AND, OR, NOT, BETWEEN, IN

Filtering

📌 Definition & Meaning: Filters atomic records before aggregations occur. Logical operators chain multiple business criteria together.

⚡ Runnable Business Example:
SELECT order_id, customer_id, sales_amount 
FROM orders 
WHERE (region IN ('West', 'South'))
  AND sales_amount BETWEEN 500 AND 5000
  AND NOT order_status = 'Returned'
ORDER BY sales_amount DESC;

⚠️ Interview Trap: BETWEEN is inclusive of both boundaries (e.g. 500 and 5000 are included). In operator precedence, AND executes before OR; always wrap OR conditions in parentheses!

2. Aggregations & Grouping

GROUP BY & HAVING (The Pipeline Rule)

Core Aggregation

📌 Definition & Meaning: GROUP BY collapses rows sharing identical values into summary buckets. HAVING filters aggregated summaries after grouping occurs.

💻 Canonical Syntax:
SELECT dimension, COUNT(*), SUM(metric)
FROM table_name
WHERE row_filter = 1
GROUP BY dimension
HAVING SUM(metric) > 10000;
⚡ Runnable Business Example:
-- Find enterprise regions with over $250k revenue from delivered orders
SELECT 
    region,
    COUNT(order_id)             AS total_orders,
    ROUND(SUM(sales_amount), 2) AS total_revenue,
    ROUND(AVG(sales_amount), 2) AS avg_order_value
FROM orders
WHERE order_status = 'Delivered'       -- Step 1: Discard unfulfilled rows
GROUP BY region                       -- Step 2: Bucket remaining rows
HAVING SUM(sales_amount) > 250000;     -- Step 3: Filter calculated sums

⚠️ Interview Trap: COUNT(*) counts rows including NULLs; COUNT(col) counts only non-null records. AVG() ignores NULLs! Never use column aliases in a WHERE clause because WHERE executes before SELECT.

🔗 Cross-Tool: Excel: Pivot Table (Rows & Values) | Pandas: df.groupby('region').agg({'sales_amount': 'sum'})

3. Table Joins & Set Combinations

INNER, LEFT, SELF JOIN & UNION ALL

Relational

📌 Definition & Meaning: Joins stitch normalized tables horizontally along common keys. UNION ALL stacks identical schemas vertically without performing deduplication sorts.

⚡ Runnable Business Example: Detect Zero-Purchase Accounts
-- Isolate churned or registered customers who have never placed an order
SELECT 
    c.customer_id, 
    c.customer_name, 
    c.created_at,
    o.order_id
FROM customers c 
LEFT JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id 
WHERE o.order_id IS NULL;

⚠️ Interview Trap (Accidental Inner Join): Placing right-table filter conditions in the WHERE clause converts a LEFT JOIN into an INNER JOIN. Move right-table conditions into the ON clause to keep all left records!

4. CTEs & Analytical Window Functions

CTEs (WITH) & Ranking (DENSE_RANK, LAG)

Advanced Analytics

📌 Definition & Meaning: CTEs create clean, modular data pipelines. Window functions evaluate rankings, cohort shares, and historical lags without collapsing raw rows into summaries.

⚡ Runnable Business Example: Top 2 Products Per Category
WITH ranked_products AS (
    SELECT 
        category,
        product_name,
        revenue,
        DENSE_RANK() OVER (
            PARTITION BY category 
            ORDER BY revenue DESC
        ) AS rank_in_cat
    FROM product_performance
)
SELECT category, product_name, revenue
FROM ranked_products
WHERE rank_in_cat <= 2;
⚡ Month-over-Month Revenue Growth with LAG():
SELECT 
    order_month,
    monthly_sales,
    LAG(monthly_sales, 1) OVER (ORDER BY order_month) AS prev_month_sales,
    ROUND(
        (monthly_sales - LAG(monthly_sales, 1) OVER (ORDER BY order_month)) 
        / NULLIF(LAG(monthly_sales, 1) OVER (ORDER BY order_month), 0) * 100, 
        2
    ) AS mom_growth_pct
FROM monthly_summary;

⚠️ Interview Trap: Window functions are strictly forbidden in WHERE and HAVING clauses. You must calculate them inside a CTE or subquery first, then filter the result in the outer query.

5. Data Cleaning & Null Handling

COALESCE, CAST, NULLIF & CASE WHEN

Data Hygiene
⚡ Runnable Business Example: Production Hygiene
SELECT 
    order_id,
    UPPER(TRIM(city)) AS cleaned_city,
    COALESCE(work_phone, mobile_phone, 'Unreachable') AS primary_phone,
    -- Division by zero safety
    ROUND(refund_amount / NULLIF(total_sales, 0) * 100, 2) AS refund_pct,
    -- Conditional business categorization
    CASE 
        WHEN total_sales >= 10000 THEN 'Enterprise'
        WHEN total_sales >= 2000  THEN 'Mid-Market'
        ELSE 'Standard'
    END AS tier
FROM raw_orders;

Engine Logical Order of Execution (Why Aliases Fail)

1. FROM / JOIN → 2. WHERE → 3. GROUP BY → 4. HAVING → 5. SELECT → 6. WINDOW → 7. DISTINCT → 8. ORDER BY → 9. LIMIT
